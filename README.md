Rationale and Objectives: County-level mammography rates are associated with socioeconomic, racial, and healthcare-access factors, but the direction and strength of these associations are inconsistent across the United States. This cross-sectional, county-level analysis sought to formally test whether covariate-mammography associations vary across space and at what spatial scale they operate, using multiscale GWR and Monte Carlo permutation testing.

Materials and Methods: Biennial mammography rates for women aged 40–74 were obtained from National Cancer Institute small-area estimates and modeled against nine covariates from County Health Rankings, USDA, U.S. Census/American Community Survey, and FDA facility data: primary care physician (PCP) density, insurance, high school completion, income inequality, broadband access, Black and Hispanic population proportion, rurality (Rural–Urban Continuum Code), and mammography facilities per 100,000 women aged 40–74. Ordinary least squares, generalized additive models, simulation–extrapolation, Moran's I, local indicators of spatial association (LISA), and geographically weighted regression (GWR and multiscale GWR) were performed.

The code included in this repository is the same code used to clean and analyze the data (OLS, GAM, Moran, LISA). It was used to produce a cleaned dataset in an OSF repository ([DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/U9Y24](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/U9Y24)) and that dataset was analyzed by MGWR 2.2.1 GUI. This code was used to analyze the MGWR output and map it. 

Results: Multiscale GWR improved model fit over global regression (R² 0.18→0.28). Most predictors operated at a near-national scale; PCP density had a regional bandwidth while the intercept had a more local bandwidth. Monte Carlo testing showed no significant spatial variability for any predictor except intercept. In all counties, insurance and black population proportion were significant positive predictors, and facility rate and Hispanic population proportion were significant negative predictors. PCP density was significantly positive only in the Western, Central, and Southeastern U.S, but without significant spatial variability.

Conclusion: Most covariate–mammography associations were significant and spatially stable nationwide, suggesting national interventions may be beneficial without geographic targeting. PCP density may benefit from region-specific programs.
